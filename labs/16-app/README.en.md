# Lab 16: App and final experiment

**The project's question:** does correcting the documents with the traffic light **before** indexing
them improve the RAG's answers?

## 1. The app

Everything from the previous labs, put together and usable (`app/`):

```bash
uv run uvicorn app.main:app      # http://localhost:8000
uv run pytest                    # full flow with a fake evaluator, no models
```

| Screen | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Documents** (Documentos) | Upload a PDF, HTML or Word file. It is parsed in a separate process (Docling for PDF, an in-house parser for HTML and Word, as lab 11 decided) and saved as version 1. |
| **Document** | The text with each block colored and a counter ("4 rojos · 7 amarillos", i.e. 4 red · 7 yellow). "Evaluar" (Evaluate) runs the traffic light with live progress (SSE). "Indexar" (Index) appears only when no blocking reds remain. |
| **Review** (Revisión) | Jumps to the first red. It shows the rule, its standard and what the red means, and asks what is missing. The person answers; an LLM rewrites only that block; it is re-evaluated and the before and after are shown word by word. If the rewrite brings numbers that were neither in the block nor in the answer, it cannot be accepted (lab 15). Shortcuts: `n` next, `p` previous, `Enter` accept, `Esc` skip. |
| **Chat** | Answers only from indexed documents, with streaming, and lists the cited fragments. |
| **Rules** (Reglas) | The rules with their standard. Writing a new one in natural language compiles it (lab 14) or asks for a criterion if it is vague. |

Decisions made based on what the labs measured:
- **Evaluator**: Qwen3.5-4B via local logprobs (lab 13: performs the same as the 9B with half the
  memory). In the app with a single option order, for speed; offline, with two.
- **Which rules each block is checked against**: cheap filters (R6/R8 only where there are amounts, R7 where there are
  dates, R3 where there are links, R2 in tables…). Without them, a 100-block document needs
  about 800 model passes.
- **Review guardrail**: the deterministic one (new numbers). The evaluator one was left
  out because it gave false alarms (lab 15).
- **Each correction is a new version** (SQLite): the original and the history (which rule, what
  the person answered) are not lost; the verdicts of the other blocks are reused.
- **Retrieval**: dense + re-ranking (labs 04 and 05), without BM25.

### Test with the real models

With the City (of Buenos Aires) driver's license fees page (`data/raw/corpus/caba-licencias-aranceles.html`):

1. Upload and parsing: under one second.
2. Traffic light: 49 evaluations in 59 s. It flagged, among others, 3 amounts without an effective date (R8) and **the
   whole document in red for R9**: the page does not say who publishes it or when (which is true).
3. R9 review: the person answers "lo publica el Gobierno de la Ciudad (Dirección General
   de Habilitación de Conductores), actualizado el 2 de marzo de 2026" (it is published by the City Government, Directorate General of Driver Licensing, updated March 2, 2026) → a sentence is added
   at the beginning → R9 turns green (28 s, with a fallback model because Groq had run out of quota).
4. R8 review on "$42.230,00" → "$42.230,00 (rige desde el 1 de enero de 2026, según la
   Ley Tarifaria 2026)" (in force since January 1, 2026, according to the 2026 Tariff Law) → green (2 s).
5. Chat: "¿Quién publica la información de aranceles y cuánto cuesta el CENAT?" (Who publishes the fee information and how much does the CENAT cost?) → it answers
   both facts with two citations, with streaming. **The first one can only be answered because
   R9 was corrected**: the original document did not say it.

Found in this test and fixed: document-scope problems (R9, R10) blocked
indexing but did not appear in the review, so a document without an issuer could never be
indexed. Now the review includes them and the correction adds a sentence at the beginning.

What the app does not do yet (and why):
- **R1 (color)**: the parser does not keep the text style (SDD, risk 3).
- **R5, R11 and R12** are not evaluated in this version.
- Deterministic rules generated by the compiler: the compiler does not generate code (lab 14).

## 2. The experiment

### Method

Two corpora with the same RAG pipeline, the same model and the same judge:

- **A**: the 49 documents as they come out of parsing.
- **B**: the same ones, after going through the traffic light and the guided review.

Steps (`experiment.py`, each one can be resumed):

1. **Traffic light**: R6 (currency) and R8 (effective date) on the 60 blocks with amounts from the documents
   used by the questions, with the 4B evaluator and two option orders.
2. **Automatic guided review** of the reds. The "person" who answers is an LLM acting
   as the **document's author**: it only sees the sentences with dates from its own document, and if the
   fact is not there, it answers "no sé" (I don't know). Another LLM rewrites the block; the deterministic guardrail
   rejects numbers that are not in the document.
3. **RAG over A and over B**, comparing the questions where the corpora can differ: the 8 from the
   general set whose evidence is in a corrected document, 7 new effective-date questions
   ("¿desde cuándo rige la tasa de $154.000…?", i.e. since when is the $154.000 fee in force…?, `vigencia_questions.jsonl`) and the 10
   unanswerable ones.

Answers and judge: **local Qwen3.5-9B**. It was planned with Qwen3.8-27B on Groq, but the free
tier has a cap of **200,000 tokens per day per model** and the labs of that day
exhausted it on both Groq models. Since A and B use the same model and the same judge, the
comparison remains fair.

```bash
uv run labs/16-app/experiment.py --phase gate
uv run labs/16-app/experiment.py --phase review
uv run labs/16-app/experiment.py --phase rag
uv run labs/16-app/experiment.py --phase report
```

### Results

Everything, with rewrite examples, in [`results.md`](results.md).

| Stage | Result |
|---|---|
| Traffic light, R8 effective date | 43 reds out of 60 blocks with amounts |
| Traffic light, R6 currency | 0 reds, 56 yellows (only the $ sign) |
| Review | 40 reviewed (3 were left unreviewed due to the quota), 33 corrected, 3 marked [FALTA], 1 rejected for new numbers |

| Questions | A, correct | B, correct |
|---|---|---|
| 8 from the general set (evidence in a corrected document) | 7 | 7 |
| 7 effective-date ones | 6 | 6 |
| 10 unanswerable: abstains | 10 | 10 |

Two questions changed, in opposite directions:
- **v07 improved.** In the IOMA document, each date ("A partir del 01/06/2026", i.e. from 06/01/2026) and its amount
  ("$ 105.037") were in separate blocks, and with A the model paired them wrong: it answered August
  instead of June. The correction put the date together with its amount in the same block.
- **v02 got worse.** Its block was one of the 3 that did not get corrected; the other corrected
  chunks from the same guide probably pushed the fragment with the effective date out of the top 5
  (not verified), and the model abstained.

### Conclusion

1. **In this corpus, correcting before indexing did not improve the answers: a tie.** The project's
   hypothesis is not demonstrated. With 15 comparable questions, a 13-to-13 tie does not prove
   that there is no effect, but there is no sign of a large one either.
2. **Why: the RAG compensates for what the traffic light flags.** R8 flagged 43 blocks without an effective date, and
   it is true that the block does not have it. But the fee guide repeats "Vigencia para documentos
   ingresados a partir del 01/01/26" (effective for documents submitted from 01/01/26) in every section: retrieval brings that fragment along
   with the amount's fragment and the model connects them. In A it already answered 6 of 7 effective-date questions. The
   traffic light evaluates the block in isolation; the RAG reads several blocks together. **What is red for a
   reader who jumps to that paragraph is not necessarily red for the RAG.**
3. **Where the correction did help was in the structure, not in the data.** v07 improved because the
   review put back together a date and its amount, which parsing had separated (the same
   problem that appeared in labs 02 and 11). That points to another version of the product: a
   traffic light that detects **misaligned data** (amounts without their concept or their date in the same
   block) rather than writing rules.
4. **Corrections have side effects on retrieval.** Changing some blocks
   changes which fragments compete for the top 5, and that can make worse questions that had nothing
   to do with them (v02). Partially correcting a document can be worse than not touching it.
5. **Rewrites remain risky even without inventing numbers**: "se verá impactado en
   la cuota del mes de abril 2026" (will be reflected in the April 2026 installment) became "a partir del 1 de abril de 2026" (from April 1, 2026). Plausible, not
   identical. Human acceptance with the before and after (the review screen) is not a
   UX detail: it is the last line of defense.

6. **The value the experiment did not measure.** In the app test, the question "¿quién
   publica esto?" (who publishes this?) became answerable **only** because R9 was corrected. Rules that add
   a fact the document did not have (issuer, date, effective date missing from the whole document)
   do change what the RAG can answer; those that rearrange a fact that was already somewhere
   else do not. The experiment's question set had almost none of the first kind.

### Open problems
- **Measure the value of the traffic light with a different yardstick.** For the RAG, the writing and effective-date rules
  contribute little in this corpus; for a person who reads a single block (or a search engine that
  shows a fragment), they contribute more. A reader-centered evaluation is needed, not just one
  of the chat's answer.
- **Quotas.** With the free tier, the whole project does not fit in one day: 200,000 tokens per
  model per day on Groq, 20 requests per day on Gemini. The local model solves the cost,
  but it takes about a minute per answer on the Mac.
- **Scale of the experiment**: 15 comparable questions and a single run. To say anything
  firm, more documents with real problems and more questions that depend on them would be needed.
